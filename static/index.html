Finishing pass 

      

        
The conversion decides what can be earned.
          Nothing in it asks whether the result looks good —
          coverage and staleness are statistics over pixels, and neither notices a
          wall that is one column smeared forty times, nor a thin dark line down
          it, nor a wall that boils while the picture holds still. This is the
          pass that asks.

        
It settles the walls first, and that part is
          free. Each frame's wing is medianed against its own neighbours,
          so a wall stops re-forming every frame and any lone bad sample drops
          out. Every pixel involved is footage this camera already shot —
          nothing is invented, no model is called, and the real-wing figure above
          does not move. Only what is still faulted afterwards is worth paying a
          generator for.

        

          
          
          
        

        

        
A repaint is aimed at the defect, not sprayed over
          the wall. Dead pixels, dark lines and anything already invented
          are fair game; photography is not. An earlier version handed the whole
          wing to the model and a wall that was 99% filmed came back 37%, with a
          second copy of a logo that was already on screen — to fix
          artefacts measuring 1.5% of its columns.

        
A note you pinned in Review changes what this
          does. A fault is a blemish, so it is painted through the
          blemish. A note is a statement about what belongs on that wall —
          so it opens the whole wing, it works even on a shot the model called
          acceptable, and everything it changes lands on DIRECTED
          rather than GENERATED. It costs the real-wing figure exactly what it
          takes, stated below. Wanting something is reason enough to ask for it;
          the number just has to stay honest about it.

        
A photographed wall may be repainted too.
          A streaked recovered wing is a real defect and you should be able to fix
          it — so this refuses nothing and states the cost instead. Every
          pixel the model changes is relabelled GENERATED, or
          DIRECTED where a note you pinned in Review drove it, and
          the real-wing figure above falls by exactly the repainted share. The film
          gets better; the number stays true.
