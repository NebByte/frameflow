What it refuses, and why that is the point

Any tool can fill a wall. The measurement only means something if the tool will decline — so three gates run before anything is kept, and a shot that fails one is recorded with the number that failed it.

Geometry it cannot verify

A frame is hidden, rebuilt from its neighbours alone, and scored against the real one. Below 20 dB the shot is refused.

A rung was reporting invented pixels as photographed. DONATED accepted a donor on homography inliers alone. On aerial footage of a park — near-planar and self-similar, the worst case for that test — three of five accepted matches were different moments entirely: 70, 84 and 71 inliers, but 40.3, 31.3 and 34.8 mean absolute difference once warped back.

Inliers establish "consistent with one homography", never "the same take". The tool now warps the donor and looks at the pixels. On the drone clip that cut DONATED from 81.5% to 36.9%; on the apartment walk it left 8.4% exactly where it was. It removes the false cluster and leaves real matches alone.

The refusal that mattered most was structural. On the drone footage the 3D path returned poses it could not vouch for, and the backend raised rather than rendered. That exception escaped and killed the whole run — every other shot's verdict, and the summary itself, went with it.

A refusal is the product here, so it now lands as that shot's verdict and the film carries on. Six assertions hold it in place, including that nothing outside the frame gets labelled real on a shot that was refused.

A claim this tool withdrew

DONATED — one cut of a film donating real periphery to another — was reported at 8.4% on an apartment walk. It was wrong, and the way it was wrong is worth more than the number was.

Shot detection had split a single continuous 53.8-second take into five. The matcher then found one part of that take against another part of the same take, verified it geometrically, and labelled the pixels DONATED. They were real pixels. They were on the wrong rung: same camera, same run, no second cut involved.

A candidate cut is now confirmed by looking for correspondence across it: a real cut lands on unrelated pixels and almost nothing matches, while a fast pan is the same room from a slightly different angle. On a cut trailer it keeps 76 of 78 boundaries; on the walk it removed three of four.

Where every pixel came from

Seven rungs, in order of what they can prove. The line between rung 4 and 5 is the one the headline number depends on: above it, real photons from this place. Below it, something else wearing the same colours.

0PRIMARY The original frame, untouched and byte-identicalalways
1RECOVERED This shot's own periphery, seen earlier in the same takefired · 98.7%
2DONATED The same take in another cut, framed widerretracted
3RETRIEVED Another setup of the same location, bridged in 3D6% registered
4REFERENCED A licensed image of the place — a camera, but not this onefired · 2.3%
5DIRECTED Someone who knows the room said what is therefired · 23.1%
6GENERATED Invented. Counted as invented, alwaysfired

Generated pixels are structurally unable to overwrite recovered ones: the generator receives a read-only view and the composite is checked byte-for-byte afterwards. A tool that paints outside its hole raises rather than returns.

When there is nothing to recover

On a shot the gate refuses, the walls can still be drawn — and are then labelled invented, which is why the honesty number cannot move when you turn this on. A hosted video model fills a whole shot in one submission, because per-frame generation makes walls crawl in peripheral vision.
